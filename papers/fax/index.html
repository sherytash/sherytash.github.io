After the Fax Machine Burns: Journal Certification When Everything Is Published

Herytash, S. · Under review at Management Science (Financial Times 50; Academic Journal Guide 4*) as a Discussion Paper, in response to Bhargava et al. (2026) and Terwiesch (2026).

Ask for the manuscript

Abstract

Academic peer review is under strain. Review times in economics roughly doubled over three decades, business and economics journals sit at the slow end of the disciplines, and reviewers donate some 100 million unpaid hours per year. Large language models have collapsed the cost of writing the manuscripts that consume those hours. Recent work in this journal governs artificial intelligence inside the existing workflow; the accompanying commentary asks whether that workflow, formed when pages were scarce, should give way to publication of every submission with quality control afterward. We develop that alternative into a system a publisher could run. Every submission is published on arrival, and the open marketplace produces a public, revisable grade: a machine reading opens trading, participants price a claim on a verifiable settlement event, and the publisher reads the price into a grade with disclosed precision. We model the five parties a publication system serves. Where the publisher's own panels produce the grade, its users, author, committee, and reader, weakly prefer it to the verdict. Our marketplace inherits every comparison under one further assumption, testable on the settled sample, that its prices aggregate at least the information the panel aggregates today. Reviewer and publisher constraints turn on two magnitudes left to measurement, the worth of a public reviewing record and the price institutions pay for an evaluation layer; for a society publisher the second is arithmetic on its own accounts.
